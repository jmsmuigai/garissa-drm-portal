⏱️ Spillway Cascade — Flood Wave Timeline

Once Masinga Dam begins controlled releases, here is how the flood wave travels downstream to reach Garissa:

  • 🏗️
    Hour 0 — Masinga Release Decision
    KenGen authorizes controlled spillway release from Masinga Dam (850 m³/s). Alert issued via KMD and county offices.
  • ⏱️
    Hour 6 — Wave reaches Upper Tana
    Flood wave reaches upper Tana River station (~90km downstream). PREPARE: Gather emergency kits. Identify evacuation routes. Alert neighbors.
  • ⏱️
    Hour 12 — Kora / Adamson's Falls
    Wave reaches Kora National Reserve area (~180km). EVACUATE: Riverine communities in Tana River County must move now.
  • ⏱️
    Hour 24 — Hola Town, Tana River County
    Wave reaches Hola (~280km). GARISSA COUNTY: Activate Emergency Operations Center. Pre-position supplies.
  • ⏱️
    Hour 48 — Balambala / Fafi approaches
    Wave approaching Garissa County (~360km). ⚠️ ALL RIVERINE COMMUNITIES: EVACUATE NOW. Schools within 2km of Tana to close immediately.
  • 🚨
    Hour 72 — GARISSA TOWN AT RISK
    Flood wave reaches Garissa County core (~450km). 🚨 EMERGENCY RESPONSE: Deploy all teams. Activate evacuation shelters. Schools closed. Markets closed.
🌡️ El Niño 2026 — What to Expect

📡 ICPAC/KMD Forecast — OND 2026

  • El Niño Strength: Strong to Very Strong (ICPAC/KMD 2026)
  • Peak Period: October–December 2026 (Short Rains)
  • Indian Ocean Dipole: Potential Positive phase — amplifies Kenya rainfall by additional 30–50%
  • Expected Rainfall: 150–200% above normal in Garissa
  • Population at Risk: ~280,000 people in Garissa County
  • Historical Precedent: The 2023 El Niño displaced 32,000+ people in Garissa. 2026 forecast is stronger.

⚠️ What This Means for Garissa

  • Both the Tana River AND seasonal laghas (seasonal rivers) will flood simultaneously
  • Seven Forks dams will receive unprecedented inflow — all 5 dams likely at or near capacity
  • Dadaab refugee camp (3 blocks) at elevated risk — potential displacement of 200,000+ refugees
  • Roads (B9, A3) likely cut off — supply chains disrupted
  • Cholera, malaria and RVF (Rift Valley Fever) risk rises sharply after flooding
  • Livestock losses (camels, cattle, goats) expected in riverine grazing areas

🏘️ What Should Citizens Do? — Participatory Action Guide
🚨 If You Live Within 2km of Tana River Banks — DO THIS NOW
  • Register your household with the local Chief and DRM sub-county office
  • Identify the nearest evacuation assembly point (see green stars on map)
  • Prepare a "Go Bag": national ID, medical records, 3 days food & water, phone charger, medicines
  • Move all valuable documents and electronics to the highest floor or a safe neighbor's home
  • Make a plan with your family — who takes the children, who takes elderly relatives, which route you use
  • Move livestock to high ground before flooding — do not wait until the water arrives
⚠️ For Farmers & Agro-Pastoralists Along Tana River
  • Harvest any mature crops NOW — do not wait for floods to destroy them
  • Move stores (grain, seeds) to elevated storage — a flooded granary means total loss
  • Contact Kenya Red Cross Garissa (+254-046-2023200) to register for livestock emergency support
  • Avoid planting new crops in low-lying flood plains until after the El Niño season
  • Join community-managed water pan management groups to prepare water storage for dry periods after floods
✅ Before El Niño Season Starts (Now–September 2026)
  • Clean and raise drainage channels around your home and school
  • Stockpile 2 weeks of drinking water in sealed containers
  • Get your family vaccinated against cholera and typhoid at local health centres
  • Participate in community flood preparedness training by the County DRM office
  • Form a Community Flood Watch group — identify 2 people to monitor the river level daily
  • Spread awareness in your community using this advisory page — share the link!
