About Garissa DRM & GEWAS

The Garissa County Disaster Risk Management (DRM) Portal and the Garissa Early Warning System (GEWAS) represent a paradigm shift in how we approach disaster resilience. Born out of the necessity to protect lives, livelihoods, and critical infrastructure from increasingly volatile climatic extremes, GEWAS serves as the digital nerve center for disaster preparedness in Garissa County.

Our mandate is clear: to transition from reactive emergency response to proactive, intelligence-driven disaster anticipation. By leveraging cutting-edge satellite telemetry, hydrological modeling, and community-based participatory mapping, we provide decision-makers and citizens with the critical lead time required to save lives.

Our Vision

To create a resilient Garissa County where communities are empowered with actionable intelligence, seamlessly anticipating and withstanding natural hazards, and where disaster-induced loss of life and livelihoods is entirely eliminated.

Our Mission

To deploy state-of-the-art multi-hazard early warning systems, integrate deep community knowledge, and foster strategic partnerships that ensure rapid, effective, and empathetic disaster mitigation, response, and recovery across all sub-counties.

"We do not inherit the land from our ancestors, we borrow it from our children. Protecting Garissa from climatic extremes is our duty to the future."

The GEWAS Architecture

GEWAS (Garissa Early Warning System) is built on a foundation of spatial data infrastructure (SDI) and artificial intelligence. It integrates:

  • Earth Observation Data: Live feeds from Sentinel and MODIS satellites via Google Earth Engine to track flood extents and vegetation health.
  • Hydrological Telemetry: Real-time monitoring of River Tana levels and the Masinga Dam cascade spillway.
  • Vulnerability Mapping: High-resolution mapping of exposed populations, schools, health clinics, and vital boreholes.
  • Community Intelligence: Integrating local knowledge and participatory mapping to ensure solutions are grounded in the reality of Garissa's people.

Together, these components create a 'Digital Twin' of Garissa County's risk landscape, allowing us to model scenarios like the Super El Niño 2026 and act before the waters rise.
